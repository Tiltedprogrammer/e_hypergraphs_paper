\begin{abstract}
Just as the simply typed lambda calculus is an internal language for cartesian
closed categories, many categorical structures admit type theories whose
judgments present their morphisms. We give such a type theory for representable
semilattice-enriched multicategories. Its distributive inference rules make
multilinearity syntactic, at the cost of allowing a term to have more than one
derivation. We define substitution on derivations, prove that it is independent
of their choice up to the semilattice equations, and establish its unit,
associativity, interchange, and multilinearity laws. We then prove that the
resulting calculus presents the free representable
$\catname{SLat}$-multicategory on a multigraph.

Our motivating instance is the \emph{e-graph}, the data structure powering
\emph{equality saturation}---a rewriting technique that mitigates the phase
ordering problem by applying rewrite rules non-destructively until a fixed point
is reached. E-graphs have recently been given a categorical account as string
diagrams for semilattice-enriched cartesian categories. We present a way to
render e-graphs as terms in the cartesian version of our calculus: semilattice
join collects the representatives of an e-class, while equality saturation is
expressed by rewriting modulo the structural equations. An explicit
\textbf{let} records acyclic sharing without changing the presented category.
A recursive \textbf{letrec}, in which joins in recursive definitions remain
protected, captures cyclic e-graphs and presents the free traced cartesian
representable $\catname{SLat}$-multicategory. These constructions suggest a
foundation for programming languages with built-in equivalences.
\end{abstract}
